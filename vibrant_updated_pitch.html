01The partnership

Realizing the vision you've already built.

The hardest part of any software project — knowing exactly what it should be — is usually where things stall. You've already done it.

Working through the screens, the workflows and the details, you've defined a complete operations product: how an estimate is built, how a cut list is structured, how materials are tracked, how a shutter drawing is laid out. That is real product design, and it normally takes weeks of discovery to reach. You've reached it already.

What's left is a different craft. Inzeph's job is the engineering beneath the surface — making your tools safe for your whole team to use at once, connecting them so nothing is re-typed, and wiring them to your live data so the numbers are real. Two crafts, one product. You designed it; we make it run.

We're not redoing your work. We're building the engine under it.
What you've built

The product, fully defined

Five working tools that already cover most of a project's life — the screens, the workflows, and the domain detail of how Vibrant Spaces actually quotes, builds and procures. The "what" is done.

Each tool is a polished, single-purpose app that runs in the browser. Read together, they already cover the spine of an interior project — which is exactly why bringing them onto one platform is worth doing, rather than starting over. Here is what each one owns, and the one thing they all share: they're sealed off from each other.

04What v1 is — and isn't

Make the product real. Hold a firm edge around it.

v1 is your five tools, rebuilt as one platform — with real logins, shared data, a full change history, and a live connection to your data so the screens show real numbers instead of sample figures. We keep the product exactly as you've defined it; what we rebuild is the plumbing beneath it, smoothing the rough edges in the flow as we go. We're making your screens work for real — not redesigning what they do.

Holding a firm edge around v1 is what keeps it fast and on-budget. Anything not already in your five tools sits outside v1, by design:

  • Data intelligence & the AI design tool — analytics, forecasting and the design assistant come later, in Phase 2 (see section 09).
  • An HR / people module — a natural future extension once the platform is live, but not part of this build.
05The foundation it needs

Four things every real product needs — that the tools don't have yet.

These aren't features bolted onto one screen. They're the ground the whole platform stands on — the part that simply can't exist in a browser-only tool, and the reason this is engineering rather than wiring.

  • Real users & secure sign-in. Your whole team works in the same system at the same time, each with their own account.
  • Access control you configure. You define the roles and decide what each can see and do — and change them yourself, anytime. Nothing is hard-coded by us.
  • One shared source of truth. Every tool reads and writes the same project, so a number entered once is correct everywhere. This is what ends the re-typing.
  • A full history, with rollback. Every meaningful change records who did what and when, and any record can be restored to an earlier state.
06How it fits together

Your tools as modules, on one shared core.

The shape is simple: one thin shared core that every tool reads and writes, with your tools sitting on top as modules. The core holds the things the tools each keep a separate, broken copy of today — the client, the project, the shared libraries, the people, and the history. It's built on a modern, proven, widely-used technology stack, so it stays fast, secure, and straightforward to maintain and extend.

08The build sequence

Foundation first, then value in a deliberate order.

The order isn't arbitrary — each step depends on the one before it, so we build the foundation once, then deliver working modules as fast as the dependencies allow.

  • Foundation. The shared core, sign-in, access control and history. Nothing else is trustworthy without it.
  • Estimation. The entry point of every project — quoting on real, live rates from day one.
  • Production & procurement. They depend on an estimate existing first, and run on your real designs and materials.
  • Scheduling. The project schedule as a native module on the same record.
  • Control centre. Last, because it can only show what the earlier modules now feed it.
09Connecting to your data

What makes it a product, and not a prototype.

Your tools are built around your real operational data — your rates, your materials, your costs. Today they run on figures entered by hand. For the platform to be genuinely useful — not just a better-looking version of the manual process — its screens need to read your real, current numbers: the estimate pricing from your live rates, the production tools reflecting your actual designs and materials. That connection is what turns the prototype into a tool your team relies on daily, and it's what finally removes the re-typing between systems that makes today's tools slow.

This is a focused, bounded step — and it is deliberately not the larger "data intelligence" work, which we keep for later. The distinction matters, so it's worth being precise:

Now — part of building the product

Operational connection

Bringing your live, current data into the platform so the screens work for real and your team stops re-entering it. Specific values, read into the product — for example, from your finance system (Tally) and your design system (Naadi).

The plumbing that brings water to the taps. Without it, the taps are dry.
Later — Phase 2

Data intelligence & AI design tool

Analytics across every project, forecasting, and the AI design assistant — new capabilities built on top of the data the platform gathers over time. Powerful, but not needed for the product to function.

The analytics built on the water system — once it's already running.

Why this belongs in the build now, not in Phase 2:

  • A product on sample data is still a prototype. This connection completes the product you've built — it isn't an add-on.
  • Removing the manual re-entry is the value. Launch without it and the silos return in a nicer shell.
  • It's far cheaper to wire in as we build the screens than to retrofit afterward — and it's a bounded task, not the open-ended data work, so it doesn't stretch the timeline.

None of this blocks the direction above — it's simply what lets us start cleanly and move quickly.
